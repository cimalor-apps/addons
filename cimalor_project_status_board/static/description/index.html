Project Status Board

Know which projects are moving, waiting or on hold, and why

Project's stages tell you where a project is, not why it is stuck nor whether it will arrive. Project Status Board turns them into a board of states, asks why the moment a project stops, and shows on every card whether its hours and its delivery date will hold.

Three questions the board answers

What is it waiting for?

A project that stops is either Waiting (something close, to be chased) or On Hold (a long, agreed pause). Each one asks for a reason from a short list, not a free-text excuse.

Will the hours be enough?

Allocated hours left minus the work still planned on the open tasks. A red clock warns that the project will go over budget before it does.

Will it arrive on time?

Each person works through their tasks by due date at their real capacity. A red calendar says the project will be late, and who it waits for.

Two traffic lights per project

  • Hours margin: red over budget, amber tight (under 10 % of the allocated hours), green with room.
  • Days margin: working days between the forecast end and the delivery date. Red late, amber two days or less, green on time.
  • The forecast takes every project of the same people into account, with their working schedule, time off, public holidays and a daily admin time.
  • Grey, never green, when there is not enough data to tell: no allocated hours, no delivery date, nobody's schedule.
  • Reporting › Date Risk lists the projects at risk.

One form for the state and the work

  • The state as a row of buttons, with its reason and date.
  • The main tasks as a to-do list with planned and spent hours. Tick a task to close it; its planned hours are never touched.
  • A timer on every task: press play, and stop to log a standard timesheet line with a required comment. The latest comment shows on the card.
  • The full history of state changes: who, when, why. Never rewritten.

Workload and capacity, per person

Workload splits the pending hours by person, project state and due date. Capacity shows how many weeks of work each person has and the day their queue ends, with on-hold work apart. Each person sees their own figures; Project administrators see everybody's.

Your reasons, your words

Ten reasons come out of the box, from Reply to an email to Lack of budget. Rename them, add your own, reorder them. On Hold reasons propose who the project depends on, and catch-all reasons such as Other require a note.

Built on Project, not next to it

The columns are Project's own stages, the to-do list is Project's own tasks and the timer logs standard timesheet lines. Nothing is copied into a parallel model, and the Project app keeps working exactly as before. No dependency on Enterprise modules, and no data is sent outside your database.

Up and running in five steps

  1. Install Project Status Board. It depends on Project and Timesheets.
  2. Project stages are switched on, and two stages are added: Waiting and On Hold.
  3. Link each user to an employee with a working schedule, and adjust the Daily Admin Time next to it (1 hour by default).
  4. Give your projects allocated hours and a delivery date, and your tasks planned hours.
  5. Open Projects and drag each project to its real state.
